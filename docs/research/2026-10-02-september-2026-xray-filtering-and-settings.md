# September 2026 Xray/VLESS reports and settings survey

Scope: primary-source reports published in September 2026 and the public *settings* visible in `zieng2/wl` as of September 30. No subscription was imported, used as a proxy, probed, or saved; profile URLs, server addresses, IDs, keys, SNI values and paths are omitted. Public profile text was parsed in memory solely to aggregate non-secret fields. See the separate [veles → buyan investigation](2026-09-30-veles-buyan-xray-transport-failures.md) for this deployment's measurements.

## September reports: observations, not a universal filtering rule

- **September 8:** A Russian VPS operator [reported](https://github.com/XTLS/Xray-core/discussions/6740) increasingly strict outbound-to-foreign filtering, including connection count/intensity, and asked about initiating the cross-border tunnel from the foreign side. This is a proposed architecture and an individual field report, not evidence that reverse initiation would work on veles's route.
- **September 19:** A user of Xray v26.9.8 [reported](https://github.com/XTLS/Xray-core/discussions/6785) Beeline/MegaFon differences and transient relief on changing Chrome to Firefox. There are no packet captures or confirmed TSPU rules in that report; neither fingerprint is a reliable general remedy.
- **September 3:** An [Xray-core report](https://github.com/XTLS/Xray-core/issues/6714) reproduced a `randomized` uTLS/REALITY HelloRetryRequest failure, dependent on a fingerprint specification seeded for the life of the process. Firefox worked in that reporter's controlled comparison; the maintainer advised against `randomized`/`random`. This is a client-side defect, not necessarily censorship, and the exact handshake failure has not been captured on veles.
- **September 21–28:** [Issue #6797](https://github.com/XTLS/Xray-core/issues/6797) includes a traffic capture, a small transport reproduction and follow-up localhost tests showing that v26.9.9's Go 1.27 HTTP/2 implementation can open many physical XHTTP connections during cold or stalled handshakes despite XMUX `maxConnections`. The primary controlled tests cover TLS/H2 (including `stream-one` and `packet-up`), **not veles's REALITY profile**. Severity is disputed in the issue; do not infer that this is the cause of veles's failure or that setting `maxConnections: 1` will bound all sockets.
- **September 30:** [Issue #6876](https://github.com/XTLS/Xray-core/issues/6876) reports a gRPC redial regression in v26.9.30 *without finalmask*, after the first connection is lost, and explicitly reports v26.9.9 unaffected. Veles configures TCP finalmask; the issue is a version/config-specific upgrade warning, not evidence for the current xHTTP/TCP failures.
- A much-discussed [June issue #6293](https://github.com/XTLS/Xray-core/issues/6293) hypothesizes subnet/SNI/fingerprint and connection-burst filtering. Its commenters disagree on whether browser-fingerprint mismatch or traffic timing causes the observed blocks. The quoted numerical thresholds are **not an established TSPU specification**. Searches did not yield a directly retrievable, independently verified September 2026 ntc.party measurement for the veles→buyan route.

## `zieng2/wl`: settings only, not a connection recipe

The [README](https://github.com/zieng2/wl/blob/main/README.md) describes **mobile-network whitelist bypass**, not Russian-VPS-to-foreign-VPS transit. It says profiles are rotated hourly and recommends URL/application-level reachability tests rather than TCP/ICMP ping, plus automatic selection and, for this mobile use case, checking routing rules that bypass the tunnel. A passing TCP connect alone does not verify a usable VLESS session. The README also recommends enabling the client's “allow insecure” switch to increase available profiles; **do not copy that security choice**: [Xray's TLS documentation](https://xtls.github.io/en/config/transports/tls.html) warns it disables certificate validation, risks MITM and is deprecated in favor of certificate pinning.

The following is a count of *advertised fields*, **not measured working configurations**. A UTC September 30 [repository snapshot](https://github.com/zieng2/wl/tree/e82414c0214f42c9a82bc57542275690337546bd) has 163 entries in its universal list: 56 RAW (51 REALITY, 5 TLS), 50 XHTTP (19 REALITY, 31 TLS), 36 gRPC (all REALITY), and 21 WebSocket (all TLS). Among these entries, `fp=firefox` appears 98 times, `fp=chrome` 20 times and `fp=random` 17 times; other values are present. Of 50 XHTTP entries, 20 specify `mode=auto`, 17 `packet-up`, 3 `stream-one` and 10 omit `mode`. A separate three-entry small list advertises TCP+Vision+REALITY with `fp=chrome`, showing that even this repository does **not** regard Chrome as universally unusable. The lists overlap and must not be added together.

At the time of this review, [the main-branch tree](https://api.github.com/repos/zieng2/wl/git/trees/ed66ad9a8448a0950bc3c224394b92d8d421ff04) had 95 universal-list entries, only 3 of them XHTTP+REALITY (versus 19 on September 30). Hourly turnover, selection bias, and unknown per-network success rates make counts unsuitable for choosing veles settings. **No server/credential/SNI/path from these lists should be copied or tested.** `fp=random` and `fp=randomized` are distinct Xray settings: [the TLS reference](https://xtls.github.io/en/config/transports/tls.html) describes the former as choosing a browser preset and the latter as generating a fully randomized ClientHello. Do not treat prevalence of `random` as support for veles's `randomized` configuration.

## Implications for veles → buyan

Keep the existing working gRPC path until an isolated alternative passes from veles; [Xray's gRPC docs](https://xtls.github.io/en/config/transports/grpc.html) recommend XHTTP for *new* setups but do not make XHTTP an automatic replacement on this route. [XHTTP's design notes](https://github.com/XTLS/Xray-core/discussions/4113) describe HTTP/2 reuse and distinguish REALITY's default `stream-one` from TLS/H2's default `stream-up`; the different protocol/security combinations in the public list are not interchangeable. For controlled changes, compare fresh versus reused connections, avoid bursts, verify application-level responses and the target/SNI/certificate relationship ([REALITY documentation](https://xtls.github.io/en/config/transports/reality.html)). A failure with a fixed fingerprint, as already reproduced on veles, cannot be cured by eliminating `randomized` alone. No production configuration was changed for this survey.
